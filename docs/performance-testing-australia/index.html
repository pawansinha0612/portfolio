APRA CPS 230

CPS 230 operational resilience: where performance testing fits

APRA's Prudential Standard CPS 230 Operational Risk Management commenced on 1 July 2025 (APRA media release, 17 July 2023). An updated version of CPS 230 and its guidance, CPG 230, commences on 1 July 2026, according to APRA's operational risk page.

It applies to all APRA-regulated entities: authorised deposit-taking institutions (banks, building societies and credit unions, including foreign ADIs' Australian branches), general, life and private health insurers, and RSE licensees (super fund trustees). That's paragraph 2 of the standard (PDF).

What the standard asks for

  • IT capability "to meet its current and projected business requirements and to support its critical operations" (para 24).
  • Critical operations. At a minimum, payments, deposit-taking and management, custody, settlements and clearing for an ADI. Claims processing for an insurer. Investment management and fund administration for an RSE licensee. And for everyone, customer enquiries and "the systems and infrastructure needed to support critical operations" (para 35).
  • Tolerance levels for each critical operation: the maximum time you'd tolerate a disruption, the maximum data loss you'd accept, and the "minimum service levels" you'd maintain while running on alternative arrangements (para 37).
  • A credible business continuity plan and a "systematic testing program" that covers all critical operations, includes an annual business continuity exercise, and tests your ability to meet tolerance levels "in a range of severe but plausible scenarios", including disruptions at material service providers (paras 42 and 43).
  • Scenario analysis to assess the impact of severe operational risk events and "test its operational resilience" (para 26(c)), and regular testing of controls (para 29).
  • Material service providers: a register, formal agreements and monitoring, and APRA notification within 24 hours of a disruption to a critical operation outside tolerance (para 41).

What it doesn't say

CPS 230 doesn't mention load testing or performance testing by name, and nobody should tell you it "requires a load test". It is principles-based: it tells you what you must be able to show, not which test to run. Performance, capacity and failover testing is one practical way to produce that evidence for the technology behind a critical operation.

CPS 230 asksIT capability for current and projected business requirements (para 24)

Testing evidenceA load test at today's peak and at next year's forecast, and a capacity plan showing where the next limit is and when you will reach it.

CPS 230 asksMinimum service levels under alternative arrangements (para 37(c))

Testing evidenceLoad the system at forecast peak while it runs degraded: one data centre or availability zone, a failover database, a reduced cluster. Record whether it still meets the minimum service level you set.

CPS 230 asksSevere but plausible scenarios, including service provider disruption (paras 42–43)

Testing evidenceDemand-surge and dependency tests: a sudden traffic spike, a slow or failing payment gateway, identity provider or core system, with timeouts, retries and queues measured.

CPS 230 asksMaximum disruption time and data loss (para 37(a)–(b))

Testing evidenceFailover and recovery under realistic load, timed end to end, with in-flight transactions reconciled so you know what was lost or duplicated.

CPS 230 asksScenario analysis and impact of new products and technology (paras 25, 26(c))

Testing evidenceA capacity model with stated headroom, and a performance test before a major change, migration or new channel goes live.

This is my reading of the published standard to help you scope testing. It isn't legal or compliance advice: your risk and compliance team decides what evidence your entity needs. CPS 230 also points to CPS 234 Information Security for information security requirements (para 24). That matters to load testing mainly through test data, covered in the FAQ below.

Other Australian drivers

  • Peak retail events. Click Frenzy, Black Friday, Cyber Monday, Christmas, Boxing Day and EOFY sales concentrate a season of traffic into hours. The first Click Frenzy in November 2012 is a well-known example: shoppers spent hours unable to reach the site after the 7pm launch, and some retailers' own sites froze too (AFP via Phys.org, 21 November 2012).
  • Government deadlines. Tax time, census night and grant rounds put a whole country on one site. On 9 August 2016 the ABS took the online Census form offline just after 7.30pm, after a confluence of events that included denial-of-service activity and an overloaded router. It came back at 2.30pm on 11 August, and reviews confirmed it wasn't a hack (ABS, 2016 Census online form update).
  • Consumer Data Right. The Consumer Data Standards (version 1.36.0 at the time of writing) set non-functional requirements for data holders: 99.5% availability per month, and 95% of calls per hour answered within the endpoint's tier threshold. Thresholds are 1,000 ms for high-priority endpoints, 1,500 ms for low-priority and unauthenticated ones, 4,000 ms for unattended calls and 6,000 ms for large payloads. Traffic thresholds scale from 150 to 450 peak TPS of secure traffic by number of active authorisations, plus 300 TPS of public traffic. That is a load profile you can test against directly.

What drives the cost

  • Number of journeys and APIs. Five well-chosen journeys usually cover most of the load. Thirty means more scripting and more maintenance.
  • Protocols. HTTP and REST APIs are the quickest to script. Citrix, SAP GUI, thick clients, mainframe and messaging protocols take longer and may need LoadRunner-class tooling.
  • Environments. Whether a production-like environment exists, who books it, and how far it differs from production.
  • Test data. Synthetic accounts are fast. Masked production data, or data that is consumed by each test, takes planning.
  • Monitoring access. With APM (Dynatrace, New Relic, Datadog) and log access, diagnosis is quick. Without it, root-causing takes longer.
  • Third parties. Payment gateways, identity providers and core systems may need stubs or advance notice.

Questions

Performance testing FAQ

Does APRA CPS 230 require load testing?

Not in those words. CPS 230 requires tolerance levels for each critical operation, scenario analysis and a systematic BCP testing program using severe but plausible scenarios. Load, stress and failover tests are one practical way to produce evidence that the systems behind a critical operation can hold your minimum service levels. Your risk and compliance team decides what evidence you need.

How much does performance testing cost in Australia?

It depends on the number of journeys, protocols, environments and how much monitoring access there is. A focused test can be a fixed price agreed up front, while programme work is usually a day rate or retainer. My rate is on request: book a free call and you get a scoped quote.

How long does a load test take?

As general guidance, a focused test of a few key journeys is often one to two weeks of effort, and two to four weeks elapsed once you allow for fixes and a retest. Programmes, capacity plans and migrations take longer.

Which load testing tools do you use?

k6 is my default for new work because it lives in your repository and pipeline. I also work with JMeter, LoadRunner and Gatling. If you already own NeoLoad or another tool with working scripts, we'll weigh keeping it against moving. The method matters more than the tool.

Should we test in production or a test environment?

Usually a production-like test environment, with the differences from production written down so the results can be scaled honestly. Some questions, such as CDN, network or third-party limits, can only be answered in production.

Can you load test production safely?

Yes, with care: a quiet window you choose, a gradual ramp, abort thresholds and a kill switch, synthetic accounts that are easy to clean up, third parties told in advance, and your team watching the dashboards with me.

How do you handle customer data and privacy?

I prefer synthetic or masked data, so real customer data never needs to leave production. Test data and results stay in your environment, and load generators can run in your own cloud account and Australian region. Any copy of real data is an information asset under your security controls, including CPS 234 if you are APRA-regulated.

Do you work outside Melbourne?

Yes. I'm based in Melbourne and work remotely with teams across Australia and New Zealand. On-site work in Melbourne or Sydney is available by arrangement.
