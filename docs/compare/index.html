Checklist

How to choose a load testing tool

Answer these eight questions in order. Most teams have their answer by question three.

  1. What do you need to test? HTTP APIs and websites: any of these tools works. Citrix, SAP GUI or Oracle Forms: LoadRunner or NeoLoad. JDBC, JMS or LDAP without extensions: JMeter.
  2. Who will write and maintain the tests? Developers: pick the tool in their language (k6 or Artillery for JavaScript, Gatling for Java or Kotlin, Locust for Python). Testers who prefer a GUI: JMeter, NeoLoad or LoadRunner.
  3. Where will the tests run? In CI on every change: k6, Gatling, Artillery or Locust are the easiest. On a schedule, from a test lab: any of them.
  4. What load model do you need? Real users arrive whether your system is slow or not, so you usually want an arrival-rate (open) model. k6 and Gatling have both models built in, and Artillery is open by default.
  5. Where should the results go? Already on Grafana or Prometheus: k6 fits best. Want a full report after every run with no set-up: Gatling or JMeter.
  6. Who runs the load generators? For a few thousand users, a couple of machines you run yourself are usually enough. For many regions or very high load, a hosted service such as Grafana Cloud k6, BlazeMeter or Gatling Enterprise saves the set-up.
  7. What does your licence policy allow? k6 is AGPL-3.0, JMeter and Gatling are Apache 2.0, Locust is MIT, Artillery is MPL-2.0, and LoadRunner and NeoLoad are commercial. If in doubt, ask your legal team.
  8. What do you already have? A working library of tests and people who know the tool is worth a lot. Switch only for a clear reason.

One more thing: the tool matters less than the test. A realistic workload model, clear pass/fail goals and a production-like environment are what make a load test worth running, in any tool.
