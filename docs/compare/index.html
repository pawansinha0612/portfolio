Checklist

How to choose a load testing tool

Answer these seven questions in order. Most teams have their answer by question three.

  1. What do you need to test? HTTP APIs and websites: any of these tools works. Citrix, SAP GUI or terminal apps: LoadRunner. JDBC, JMS or LDAP without extensions: JMeter.
  2. Who will write and maintain the tests? Developers: pick the tool in their language (k6 for JavaScript, Gatling for Java or Kotlin, Locust for Python). Testers who prefer a GUI: JMeter or LoadRunner.
  3. Where will the tests run? In CI on every change: k6, Gatling or Locust are the easiest. On a schedule, from a test lab: any of them.
  4. What load model do you need? Real users arrive whether your system is slow or not, so you usually want an arrival-rate (open) model. k6 and Gatling have one built in.
  5. Where should the results go? Already on Grafana or Prometheus: k6 fits best. Want a full report after every run with no set-up: Gatling or JMeter.
  6. What does your licence policy allow? k6 is AGPL-3.0; JMeter and Gatling are Apache 2.0; Locust is MIT; LoadRunner is commercial. If in doubt, ask your legal team.
  7. What do you already have? A working library of tests and people who know the tool is worth a lot. Switch only for a clear reason.

One more thing: the tool matters less than the test. A realistic workload model, clear pass/fail goals and a production-like environment are what make a load test worth running, in any tool.
