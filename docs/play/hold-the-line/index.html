What the game is modelling

It's a toy, but every effect in it is a real one you'll meet in a load test:

  • Queueing: latency grows like 1 / (1 − utilisation). At 50% busy it barely moves; at 90% it's ten times the idle time. That's why you need headroom.
  • Caching and the CDN take load off the database and the app tier. Checkouts can't be cached, so they always reach the database.
  • Connections: connections in use = queries per second × query time (Little's law). Add app servers without a pooler and you can run the database out of connections.
  • Autoscaling lag: new servers take time to start. If your traffic jumps in seconds, autoscaling alone is too late. Scale before the sale.
  • Queues and rate limits: a checkout queue turns a database overload into a wait instead of an error, and a rate limiter keeps bots from using the capacity your customers need.

Your real system has its own numbers. The only way to know them is to measure: a peak-readiness load test finds where it breaks before your customers do.
