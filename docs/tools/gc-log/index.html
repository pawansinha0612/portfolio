JVM GC log analyser

Drop a garbage collection log and get pause times (p50 to max), throughput, Full GCs and their causes, heap before and after each collection, allocation rate, and plain-English findings such as "heap after GC keeps rising".

🔒 Runs 100% in your browser. Nothing is uploaded or stored: the log is parsed on this page, so it works for production logs you can't share.

JDK 9+ unified logging (-Xlog:gc*) from G1, Parallel, Serial, ZGC (including generational) and Shenandoah. JDK 8 -XX:+PrintGCDetails logs work best effort. Large files are fine: 100,000 lines take well under a second.

    How to capture

    Turn on GC logging (it is cheap, leave it on)

    GC logging costs almost nothing, so production JVMs should always have it. Rotate the files so they don't fill the disk.

    JDK 11, 17, 21 and later (unified logging)

    -Xlog:gc*:file=gc.log:time,uptime,level,tags:filecount=5,filesize=20m

    In containers, write to a mounted volume, or to stdout with -Xlog:gc*:stdout:time,uptime,level,tags and pull it with kubectl logs. gc alone (instead of gc*) also works here; gc* adds heap and phase detail.

    JDK 8

    -Xloggc:gc.log -XX:+PrintGCDetails -XX:+PrintGCDateStamps -XX:+PrintGCTimeStamps
-XX:+UseGCLogFileRotation -XX:NumberOfGCLogFiles=5 -XX:GCLogFileSize=20M

    JDK 8 logs are parsed best effort (Parallel, CMS and G1 pause lines). Multi-line CMS output that interleaves with other events may be partly skipped.

    Already running without logging?

    On JDK 9+ you can switch it on without a restart: jcmd <pid> VM.log output=gc.log what=gc* decorators=time,uptime,level,tags.

    What to look for

    Reading a GC log

    1. Long pauses, not average pauses. Every request in flight waits for a stop-the-world GC pause. Compare p99 and max with your latency target, and line the longest pauses up with slow-request timestamps.
    2. Full GCs and their cause. On G1, ZGC and Shenandoah a Full GC is a fallback: the collector could not keep up. The cause in brackets (Metadata GC Threshold, System.gc(), Allocation Failure) says where to look.
    3. Heap after GC over time. The low points of the heap chart are your live data. Flat is healthy. A floor that keeps rising across a long run is the classic shape of a memory leak; a cache filling up looks the same at first, so confirm with heap dumps.
    4. Throughput. The share of time the application was not paused. Under 95% is worth tuning; under 90% users feel it.
    5. Allocation rate. How fast the application creates garbage. High rates mean frequent young collections; reducing allocation often beats tuning flags.

    When the problem is not the GC

    Short pauses but slow requests? Look at threads instead: an exhausted connection pool, a saturated thread pool or lock contention show up in thread dumps. Try the thread dump analyser.

    What this can't tell you

    Findings are hedged on purpose: a log shows symptoms, not causes. A rising heap floor might be a leak or a warming cache; long pauses might be the collector or a CPU-starved container. Confirm with heap dumps, container CPU throttling metrics and load-test results from the same window.
